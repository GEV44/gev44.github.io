01

Website lead qualification

Every website enquiry is scored, logged and the hot ones reach your phone in seconds.

The business problem
Enquiries from the contact form sit in an inbox until someone reads them, and the serious ones wait as long as the weak ones.
What it does
  1. A form on your site posts the enquiry to an n8n webhook.
  2. An LLM scores intent and fit from 1 to 10 and returns a structured answer: score, priority, reason, next step.
  3. The result is written as a new row in Google Sheets.
  4. If the lead is marked hot, a Telegram message reaches you straight away.
  5. The webhook answers the form so the visitor sees a confirmation.
Tools and nodes
WebhookLLM chain with output schemaGoogle SheetsIfTelegramRespond to Webhook
What you need
Credentials, selected in n8n by name: OpenAI, Google Sheets, Telegram. A sheet tab named Leads with the columns name, email, company, message, score, priority, reason, next_step, received_at; your sheet ID and Telegram chat ID.
Flow
Main
Website form→Lead webhook→Qualify lead (LLM)→Build row→Sheets append→Hot lead?
Hot
Telegram alert→Respond OK
Not hot
Respond OK

02

Gmail triage with reply drafts

The inbox sorts itself and every new lead already has a reply draft waiting.

The business problem
Sales enquiries, support questions, invoices and spam arrive in one inbox, and the person reading it spends the first hour sorting.
What it does
  1. A Gmail trigger picks up each new message.
  2. An LLM returns a category (lead, support, invoice, spam) with a confidence value.
  3. A switch applies the matching Gmail label.
  4. For leads, a second LLM writes a reply and saves it as a draft in the same thread.
  5. Nothing is sent automatically; you read the draft and press send.
Tools and nodes

What you need
Credentials, selected in n8n by name: OpenAI, Gmail. Four Gmail labels (lead, support, invoice, spam), selected in the label nodes.

03

Telegram FAQ assistant with human handoff

Customers get instant answers from your FAQ and a human steps in when they ask.

The business problem
The same ten questions arrive in your messenger all day, and the questions that need a person get buried among them.
What it does
  1. A Telegram bot receives the customer message.
  2. If the text contains a handoff keyword, staff are notified and the customer is told a person will reply.
  3. Otherwise the workflow fetches your FAQ from a URL.
  4. An AI agent with per-chat memory (last 8 messages) answers strictly from that FAQ.
  5. The answer is sent back in the same chat.
Tools and nodes
Telegram TriggerIfHTTP RequestAI AgentWindow buffer memoryTelegram
What you need
Credentials, selected in n8n by name: OpenAI, Telegram. A bot token from BotFather, your FAQ hosted as plain text or markdown at a URL, and the staff chat ID.
Flow
Handoff
Telegram message→Keyword found?→Notify staff→Tell customer
Answer
No keyword→Fetch FAQ→Prepare context→FAQ agent + memory→Send answer

04

Booking with availability check

Booking requests are checked against your availability sheet and confirmed automatically.

The business problem
Customers ask for a time slot, someone checks the calendar by hand, and double bookings happen when two requests overlap.
What it does
  1. A booking request arrives at a webhook with name, e-mail, slot and service.
  2. The workflow reads your availability sheet and checks the requested slot.
  3. If the slot is available it is marked booked in the sheet.
  4. The customer gets a confirmation e-mail and you get a Telegram alert.
  5. If the slot is taken, the webhook answers with up to three alternative slots.
Tools and nodes
WebhookGoogle SheetsCodeIfGmailTelegramRespond to Webhook
What you need
Credentials, selected in n8n by name: Google Sheets, Gmail, Telegram. A sheet tab named Availability with the columns slot, status and booked_by, one row per bookable slot.
Flow
Available
Booking webhook→Read sheet→Check slot→Available?→Mark booked→E-mail customer→Telegram alert→Respond confirmed
Taken
Slot taken→Respond 409 + alternatives

05

Daily competitor and news digest

A short morning digest of what competitors and your industry did, without opening ten tabs.

The business problem
Keeping an eye on competitors and industry news means a daily round of tabs that is skipped as soon as the week gets busy.
What it does
  1. A schedule trigger runs every morning at 08:00 in the workflow timezone.
  2. It reads a news RSS feed and fetches a competitor page in parallel.
  3. The two sources are merged into one input.
  4. An LLM writes a five-bullet digest with one suggested action.
  5. The digest is delivered to Telegram.

06

Customer review analyzer

Every review is tagged by sentiment and topic, and you get a weekly report on what customers think.

The business problem
Reviews pile up in a spreadsheet or inbox, and patterns only become visible when someone has time to read them all.
What it does
  1. A Google Sheets trigger fires on each new review row.
  2. An LLM returns sentiment, topic, urgency and a one-line summary.
  3. The analysis is stored in a second tab.
  4. Urgent negative reviews trigger an immediate Telegram alert.
  5. Every Monday a second branch aggregates the last 7 days and an LLM writes the weekly summary, sent to Telegram.
Tools and nodes

What you need
Credentials, selected in n8n by name: OpenAI, Google Sheets, Telegram. Two sheet tabs: Reviews (review, author, date) and Analysis (review, author, sentiment, topic, urgency, summary, analysed_at).
Flow

Weekly
Every Monday→Read analysis→Aggregate 7 days→Write summary (LLM)→Send summary

Import

How to import into n8n

  1. Download the workflow JSON from its card.
  2. In n8n open Workflows, then Import from file, and choose the file.
  3. Open each node that shows a credential warning and select your own credential.
  4. Replace the placeholders marked YOUR_... (sheet ID, chat ID) and create the sheet tabs listed on the card.
  5. Run it once with sample input, check the output of each node, then switch the workflow to Active.
