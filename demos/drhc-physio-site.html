Therapies on offer

Tap a therapy for the clinic's own description.

Exercise therapy

The most common treatment. Moderate exercises under a physiotherapist's guidance keep muscles strong and flexible, support alignment and function, and reduce the chance of the same injury coming back.

Manual therapy

Hands-on work: massage, mobilization and manipulation to relax tissue, reduce pain and improve flexibility in the affected area.

Shockwave therapy

A non-invasive option. High-energy acoustic pulses are delivered to the painful area to stimulate healing. The clinic offers focused and radial shockwave and describes uses such as plantar fasciitis, tennis elbow, calcific tendinitis of the shoulder and patellar or Achilles tendinopathy.

Dry needling

Fine filament needles are placed in tight muscles or trigger points to reduce pain. It is often combined with other treatments.

Electrotherapy

Electrical stimulation to ease tissue pain and swelling. Because its benefits are short term, it is usually paired with other methods.

Kinesiotaping

Kinesiology tape on the affected area to modulate pain, improve myofascial dysfunction and limit swelling for patients who need continuous support.

Two articles worth reading first

Summaries of what the clinic itself publishes. They are general information and not a diagnosis.

Heel pain and plantar fasciitis

The clinic's article says around 1 in 10 people get plantar fasciitis, and 10 to 15 percent of them need a medical practitioner. It is inflammation of the band-like tissue between heel and toes, usually worst with the first steps in the morning and easing as you keep walking. Sometimes a calcium deposit called a calcaneal spur is involved.

For pain younger than three months the article suggests rest, regular stretching of the plantar fascia, calf stretches and comfortable footwear, and stepping on a cushion in the morning before putting weight on the foot.

Back pain: is it serious?

The article notes that 8 out of 10 people will have low back pain at some point and that it often returns. Long sitting, poor posture and little exercise are named as common contributors, and the number of young people affected is rising.

Its main message is that not every sharp pain means tissue damage: the nervous system can behave like a car alarm that goes off when someone merely leans on the car. It also addresses when to see a doctor and which symptoms to watch for, which the physiotherapists go through with you in person.

Common questions

Do I need a referral?

The site does not say. The team will confirm when you call.

Is shockwave therapy surgery?

No. The clinic describes it as a non-surgical option that uses no medication.

Can the clinic treat my back pain?

Back pain is one of the clinic's listed areas, and the site has an article on when back pain needs a doctor. Whether your own pain needs treatment, tests or urgent care is for the clinicians to decide after seeing you.

Which languages are spoken?

The website is in English and Arabic and lists Russian-speaking and Persian-speaking doctors. Ask the team who is available for physiotherapy.

What does a session cost?

Prices are not published for physiotherapy. The team gives them on request.

Can I message the clinic on WhatsApp?

Yes. The site links WhatsApp on the main clinic number, +971 4 279 8200.

Is physiotherapy the only thing DRHC does?

No. DRHC is a day-case hospital with many specialties, from orthopedic and spinal surgery to urology, ENT and dermatology. This page covers the physiotherapy centre.

What if my insurer is not on the list?

Call the team. The list on the site may not be the full picture, and they can tell you whether your plan applies.

How do the surgeons and physiotherapists work together?

The clinic says they work closely as a rehab team, which matters most for post-surgical patients recovering from spinal or knee operations.

Does the assistant on this page book me in?

No. It collects the details of a request inside this demo and sends nothing. The real booking happens when you call or message the team.
