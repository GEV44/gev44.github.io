Joint replacement and sports

Joint replacement therapy

A designated joint replacement clinic on the ground level with ample free street parking nearby. Before surgery, simple home adjustments and pre-surgery exercises for strength, balance, endurance and range of motion can speed up recovery. After surgery, the hospital physiotherapist will usually recommend an outpatient appointment 10 to 14 days later.

Sports injury and conditioning

Every physiotherapist works with amateur and elite athletes and is personally involved in sport. Rehab includes sport-specific drills, core stabilisation and strengthening, and pre-season screening to spot muscle imbalances before they cost you games. The physiotherapists work closely with trainers and coaches so everyone involved is on the same page, and rehab aims to get you back to the level you were competing at, with rehabilitative and preventative exercises side by side.

After an injury, in sport or outside it, the goal is the same: recover, then return safely to the activity you love.

Ergonomics and prevention

Ergonomics is about making the tasks, equipment and environment suit each person: safe lifting, posture, screen height, seating position and adaptive equipment. Poor ergonomics can lead to acute injuries or repetitive strain, and the physiotherapists can assess your workplace or home. At-home services go through OT Works.

The clinic also runs community talks, such as a kids' soccer injury prevention discussion, and shares articles on topics like choosing the right pillow, the hazards of sedentary behaviour and low back pain explained.

Before and after a joint replacement

Joint replacement is most often needed because of osteoarthritis, where the cartilage on the ends of the bones breaks down and causes stiffness and pain. Other causes include rheumatoid arthritis, bone infection, or a lack of blood supply to the bone after disease or trauma.

Before surgery

Adjust your home to reduce fall risk and make daily tasks easier
Do pre-surgery exercises for strength, balance, endurance and range of motion
Talk to your physiotherapist about home set-up and a pre-surgery routine

After surgery

Good to know

Can the clinic bill my insurance directly?

Yes, the clinic can direct bill most insurers. The list on the Rates page includes Pacific Blue Cross, Sun Life, Manulife, Green Shield, Medavie Blue Cross, Canada Life, Desjardins and more.

Which cards do you accept?

Visa, Mastercard, debit and cash. American Express is not accepted.

I have an ICBC or WorkSafeBC claim. What do I pay?

No user fee for physiotherapy with an approved claim. The same applies to ICBC chiropractic and to WorkSafeBC / ICBC kinesiology and active rehab.

Where do I park?

There is free street parking in the area. With the upgrades around the block it can be tight, so arrive about 10 minutes early. Nearest bus stops are Main & 31st and Main & 33rd.

What happens after a joint replacement?

The hospital physiotherapist will usually recommend an outpatient appointment 10 to 14 days after surgery. Main Street is a designated joint replacement clinic on the ground level; for OrthoRecon funding options, contact the clinic.

Is treatment one-on-one?

Yes. One-on-one sessions in private rooms or in the on-site gym, with education, exercise prescription and hands-on treatment.

Getting here

By car

The clinic sits on the ground level of Main Street between 32nd and 33rd Avenues, with free street parking in the area. Recent upgrades around the block have made parking tighter, so the team asks you to arrive 10 minutes early. They know this is a challenge for some patients and apologise for the inconvenience, which is out of their hands.

By bus, bike or on foot

The nearest bus stops are at Main & 31st and Main & 33rd. The clinic is easy to reach by walking, cycling or public transit, in the heart of the Riley Park neighbourhood near Queen Elizabeth Park and Little Mountain.
