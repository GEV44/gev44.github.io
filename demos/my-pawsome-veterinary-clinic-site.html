The vets

Two vets, one shared idea

The clinic was founded by Emi and Genevieve, two veterinary surgeons who met in Skerries.

Dr. Emi Capurro

Veterinary surgeon · cardiology and imaging

More than two decades of experience. She graduated in Italy in the early 2000s, moved to Dublin and has practiced here ever since, including ten years at Raheny Veterinary Clinic and a period as Clinical Director across three clinics in North County Dublin. An experienced ultrasonographer with a Second Level Master in Small Animal Cardiology from Pisa University.

Dr. Genevieve Driane

Veterinary surgeon · internal medicine and behavior

From Belgium, who spent part of her college years in Spain as an Erasmus student. She makes sure there is enough time with clients to explain and reassure, in a compassionate but straightforward way. Special interest in animal behavior, and currently working toward a GP Certificate in Endoscopy and Endosurgery.

They imagined a place that felt like home, built on trust, warmth and expert care. Every detail of the clinic has been studied to create a peaceful environment that helps pets, and their humans, feel at ease from the moment they walk in.
Special care

Care that fits your pet's life

Telemedicine

For registered clients, a remote consultation where the vets can:

  • Discuss symptoms or behavior
  • Review photos or videos of post-surgery sites
  • Give guidance and follow-up advice
  • Decide whether an in-person visit is needed

Rabbits, ferrets and rodents

Prey animals hide weakness, so regular check-ups catch problems early.

  • Detailed history of diet and housing
  • Annual vaccines and regular dental checks
  • Bloodwork, x-rays and parasite checks
  • Surgery when needed, including dental and reproductive

Preventative wellcare

Annual or semi-annual checkups to monitor health and address concerns early.

  • Bloodwork for organ function and infection markers
  • Bordetella and feline leukemia vaccines by lifestyle
  • Advice on dental care, nails and ears
  • Nutrition, weight and behavior tips

End-of-life and grief support

A gentle, unhurried consultation comes before any decision.

  • Palliative care at home with pain management
  • Euthanasia at the clinic or at home
  • Memorial keepsake and individual cremation
  • Pet grief counseling on request

If your pet may be in an emergency

The clinic lists these as signs to seek attention for right away. Please call 01 2634080 first.

  • Trauma, bites or a wound that will not stop bleeding
  • Rapid, labored or noisy breathing, or choking
  • Weakness or sudden collapse
  • Seizures or loss of consciousness
  • Repeated vomiting or diarrhea with lethargy
  • A bloated abdomen, or straining to urinate
What to expect

A wellness visit, step by step

Every pet is unique, so the vets customize each wellness plan to lifestyle, age and risk factors.

  1. A hands-on physical examA complete evaluation to detect early signs of health concerns.
  2. Laboratory testingBloodwork to monitor organ function, blood counts and infection markers, and to screen for diseases.
  3. Vaccines that fit your petAnnual vaccinations, plus lifestyle-specific protection such as Bordetella (kennel cough) or feline leukemia for cats.
  4. Parasites checked and preventedPrevention and treatment for fleas, ticks and intestinal parasites, with regular stool analysis.
  5. Guidance for homeDental care, nail trimming, ear cleaning, nutrition, weight management and behavior tips for between visits.
Pawsome Health Plan A monthly subscription that helps spread out the cost of routine care, a budget-friendly wellness package. Ask the team how it works for your pet.
For rabbit and rodent owners

Small animals hide illness

As prey species, rabbits and most rodents hide weakness, so visible symptoms often appear only when a disease is advanced. The clinic suggests watching for small changes.

Eating less or refusing food
Reduced activity or unusual quietness
Changes in urine or stool
Different behaviour toward people or the environment

If you notice any of these, call the clinic and the team will tell you what to do next.

Quick answers

Before you call

Are you open on Sundays?

No, the clinic is closed on Sundays. It is open Monday to Friday 8.30am to 6.30pm and Saturday 10am to 1pm.

How do I register?

Use the Register page on the clinic site. Registered clients can also use telemedicine for minor concerns.

Do you see rabbits and small animals?

Yes, there is an exotic and pocket pet service for ferrets, rabbits and small rodents.

Is there a payment plan for routine care?

The clinic offers a monthly Pawsome Health Plan that spreads out the cost of routine care. The team confirms the details.

Do you do dentistry?

Yes. Comprehensive dentistry covers dental cleaning and oral health, which helps prevent infections, pain and even serious organ damage.

Is there a cardiology service?

Yes. The Cardiology Referral Centre is led by Dr. Emi Capurro and is dedicated to diagnosis, staging and management of cardiovascular disease in companion animals.

What can telemedicine help with?

Minor concerns, post-treatment check-ins and ongoing management of chronic conditions, for clients who are registered with the clinic.

Can I get a second opinion?

Yes. The clinic offers second opinions and specialist referrals with clear explanations.
