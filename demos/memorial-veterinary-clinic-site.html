    Boarding & grooming

    A stay with a vet team next door.

    If a medical problem comes up during a stay, the clinic's veterinarians treat it.

    Boarding

    • Separate areas for cats and dogs
    • Spacious, temperature-controlled area
    • Drop-off and pick-up whenever the office is open
    • Reservations preferred, especially holidays; last-minute stays often possible
    • Dogs: distemper/parvo, rabies and Bordetella up to date
    • Cats: feline distemper and rabies up to date

    Grooming

    • Onsite dog and cat grooming
    • Bath, brush and coat trim; nails if long
    • Help with de-matting and de-shedding
    • Medicated shampoos for dry skin and allergies
    • Young puppy or kitten? Come early so grooming feels normal

    Why board at a veterinary practice?

    Medical oversightMedications are given by trained veterinary professionals, and vets are on site if your pet needs attention fast.
    Vaccines in-houseIf your pet needs updated vaccines before a stay, the clinic can do them. It suggests leaving two weeks between vaccination and boarding when possible.
    Care if something comes upShould a medical problem arise during the stay, the clinic's veterinarians make sure your pet is treated appropriately.

    How often to groom? Most pets at least once a year; some breeds far more. It depends on breed, how fast the coat grows, matting and the season.

    First visit

    Before you come in.

    New clients fill in the New Client Form ahead of time.

    1. Fill in the new client form. It is on the clinic's website under Patient Forms.
    2. Bring the paperwork. Adoption or purchase records, medical history, current medications and supplements, the food brand and feeding schedule, and your questions.
    3. Dogs on a short leash, cats in a carrier. Treats are welcome.
    4. Arrive 5 minutes early for check-in, or 10 if the forms are not done yet.

    FAQ

    Common questions.

    How often should my pet have an exam?

    Yearly for most pets. Older pets, and pets over 7 with blood work, about every 6 months. Puppies and kittens are seen more often in their first year.

    Why does my pet need a dental cleaning?

    Bad breath is not normal: bacteria in the mouth cause it, and dental disease can cause pain and affect other organs. Regular cleanings help prevent it.

    What happens during a dental cleaning?

    The vets explain the procedure at the visit, and it is done under anesthesia monitored by the team. Regular cleanings let them chart dental disease over time and may mean less time under anesthesia.

    Do you offer dental care for cats as well as dogs?

    The clinic's dental care page speaks of pets generally. Call the team on (713) 304-0525 to confirm for your pet.

    When should I spay or neuter?

    The vets will discuss the right timing for your pet at a visit.

    What about fleas, ticks and heartworm?

    The clinic covers parasite screening and prevention at yearly exams. Ask the team what suits your pet.

    My pet's behavior changed. Should I come in?

    Pets hide illness, so subtle changes matter. Call the clinic and describe what you see; the team will advise.
