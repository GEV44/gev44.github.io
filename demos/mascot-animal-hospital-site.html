Inside pet care

What a routine visit covers

General wellness exam

  • Head-to-tail examination: eyes, ears, teeth, heart, lungs, joints and muscles
  • Early identification of changes that may not be obvious at home
  • Ongoing assessment of weight, mobility, skin and oral health

Vaccinations, including rabies

  • Core vaccinations with clear scheduling guidance
  • Rabies vaccination in accordance with local requirements
  • Lifestyle and exposure risks considered when timing vaccines

Microchip identification

  • A small ISO-certified chip placed under the skin
  • A unique ID that clinics and shelters can scan
  • Help with registration and keeping contact details current

Flea, tick and heartworm prevention

  • Options based on age, lifestyle and home environment
  • Education on tick checks and early warning signs
  • Plans tailored to travel and exposure risk

Puppy, kitten and breed care

  • Exams focused on growth and development
  • Vaccine schedules for vulnerable stages
  • Breed-specific plans, important for large and giant breeds

Comfort and daily care

  • Nail trimming with gentle handling for nervous pets
  • Comfort-focused pain management plans
  • Prescription diets, supplements and treats

Travelling with a pet

Start early: some documents must be completed within a specific window before departure. Within your province, current vaccination records are sufficient. Between provinces, vet-signed vaccination records are generally enough. Flying within Canada depends on the airline. International travel needs a CFIA-accredited health certificate, signed by your veterinarian and endorsed by a CFIA veterinarian, and some destinations require a rabies titre test arranged well in advance.

Surrey dog licences

Dogs over three months old in Surrey need a valid licence and must wear the tag on a collar. A maximum of three dogs can be licensed per property unless there is a hobby or commercial kennel licence.
