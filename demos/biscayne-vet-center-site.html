When it is an emergency

What happens after you call

The hospital describes a three-step emergency process, built around a phone call first.

Phone assessment

The team gathers key details and confirms the safest next step and current availability.

Arrival and triage

Critical patients may be taken directly for rapid assessment, vital signs and stabilization.

Diagnostics and plan

The veterinarian discusses recommended tests, treatment priorities, estimated costs and monitoring.

What to tell the team on the phone
  • What happened and when
  • Species, breed, age and approximate weight
  • Current symptoms and breathing status
  • Known medications or medical conditions
  • Your estimated arrival time
  • Bring medications, records and any toxin packaging
Do not give medication or induce vomiting unless a veterinarian or poison-control professional tells you to. ASPCA Poison Control: 888-426-4435 (a consultation fee may apply).

Answers before you arrive

Frequently asked questions

Is the hospital available for emergencies?

The emergency phone line is presented as available 24 hours a day. Call 305-575-1190 before traveling so the team can confirm immediate availability and prepare for arrival.

Do I need an appointment?

Routine visits should be scheduled by phone. Emergencies are handled based on urgency and veterinary availability, so call immediately.

What animals do you treat?

The hospital's public listings focus on dogs and cats. Call to ask about other species before bringing them in.

What should I bring for an emergency?

Bring current medications, available medical records, any toxin packaging or product label, and information about when symptoms began.

Do you speak Spanish?

Yes. The hospital promotes bilingual service in English and Spanish.

Concerned about your pet?

Call now. The veterinary team will help determine the next step.
