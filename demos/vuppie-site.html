Common questions

Clear answers

How do I know if I'm ready to buy a home?

It comes down to your finances, your timeline and your stage of life. Have your down payment saved, steady income, manageable debt and extra funds for closing costs (legal fees, inspections, property transfer tax and adjustments, usually another 1.5% to 4% of the purchase price). Buying rarely makes sense if you plan to move within five years.

How much do I need for a down payment in Vancouver?

That depends on the purchase price and your situation. Pete walks you through the basics early on and helps you understand what is realistic, before you start seriously looking.

Do I need to sell my current home before I buy?

Not always. There are a few ways to approach it and each has trade-offs. You talk through your timeline, risk tolerance and options, then choose what feels right.

How many homes should I expect to see before buying?

There is no right number. Some buyers find the place quickly, others take longer. Pete helps you evaluate homes properly so each showing adds clarity instead of confusion.

Will you push me to make an offer?

No. The role is to guide you, not rush you. Pete helps you see when a home is worth acting on and when it is not. The decision is always yours.

What happens if I change my mind?

That is okay. Buying a home is a big decision and clarity often comes in stages. There is no obligation to move forward just because you have started the process.

How do you help me avoid overpaying?

By slowing things down and focusing on fundamentals: pricing context, comparable sales, condition and long-term value, so decisions rest on understanding rather than pressure or headlines.

How do you decide on the listing price?

A pricing recommendation considers recent comparable sales, homes currently competing for the same buyers, your property's condition and current market activity. The asking price is part of the marketing strategy and not a guarantee of the eventual sale price.

Is real estate commission negotiable in Vancouver?

Yes. Commission is negotiated between you and the brokerage and recorded in the listing agreement. Ask about the total fee, applicable tax, included services, separate expenses and any amount offered to a buyer's brokerage before you sign. For specifics on fees, Pete will go through them with you directly.

How much does it cost to sell a home in Vancouver?

The total depends on your agreed commission, legal fees, mortgage terms, preparation and moving costs and completion adjustments. Start with a property-specific estimate so you can see the likely amount left after the sale; Pete can prepare one in a first conversation.

How long will it take to sell my Vancouver home?

Timing varies with property type, pricing, presentation and the competition when you list. Pete separates preparation, time on market and the period between accepted offer and completion, and plans around current comparable activity rather than a promised deadline.

Should I buy my next home before selling?

That depends on your financing, comfort with uncertainty and the availability of suitable homes. Selling first gives a clearer budget; buying first can create carrying costs and financing pressure. Review both timelines with your realtor and lender.

What documents should I gather before listing?

Property tax information, mortgage details, records of renovations and permits and relevant warranties. For a strata home, ask which strata records and forms are needed. Your realtor and legal adviser can identify anything else.

What should I review besides the offer price?

The deposit, subjects and deadlines, completion and possession dates, included items and any special terms. A higher price can come with conditions or timing that affect your plans.

Do I have to report the sale of my principal residence?

Yes. A principal residence exemption may shelter the gain, but reporting and designation requirements still apply. For unusual situations, ask your accountant before relying on an exemption.

Will I pay a penalty when I sell with a mortgage?

You may, depending on your mortgage terms and timing. Ask your lender for a written payout estimate tied to your expected completion date, and about porting if you plan to buy again.

Do I need to renovate or stage before selling?

Not always. The useful work depends on your home's condition, the buyers it is likely to attract and your timeline. Start with a walkthrough and a prioritized plan. Cleaning, repairs and presentation can be more practical than a major renovation.

What if my home doesn't sell?

Pete plans for that upfront and talks you through the different scenarios ahead of time, so there are no surprises and you stay in control of next steps.

Can I start planning without committing to list?

Yes. An early conversation helps you understand likely pricing, preparation and timing before deciding whether a sale makes sense. Bring your questions and your preferred timeline.

How disruptive is the selling process?

It depends on the plan you choose. Pete works with sellers to minimize disruption and keep the process predictable and manageable.
