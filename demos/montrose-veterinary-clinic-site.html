Boarding

A clean, comfortable space of their own

Pets keep their familiar, regular meals, with daily exercise, mental stimulation and human affection. You can request two 15-minute play sessions during a stay, and bathing can be scheduled at the same time.

Dog boarding is a more open, social environment, while cat boarding is calm and quiet. Worried about leaving your pet? Set up a time to visit the hospital and look around the boarding facility.

  • Bring any medication in its original, clearly labeled container
  • Bring enough of your pet's normal food for the whole stay
  • Booster shots may be needed for vaccines that are due
  • Bedding and bowls are provided

Boarding questions

Why not just a feeding visit at home?

Most pets thrive on constant company, and a pet who feels abandoned can show anxiety and behavior problems.

What amenities are offered?

Familiar meals, a clean space, daily exercise, mental stimulation and affection.

Dog or cat boarding, what is different?

Dog boarding is more open and social, cat boarding is quiet and calm.

Can my pet be bathed during a stay?

Yes. Bath, brush out, nail trim and anal gland expression can be scheduled at the same time.

Is a vaccine update needed first?

Possibly, for boosters that are due or for communicable diseases such as kennel cough. The team will confirm for your pet.

Vaccinations

Core and non-core, explained

The clinic divides vaccines into core and non-core. Which ones your pet needs depends on lifestyle and travel, and your veterinarian decides that with you.

Dogs

Core
  • Rabies
  • Parvovirus
  • Canine distemper
  • Canine hepatitis
  • Bordetella
Non-core
  • Lyme disease
  • Infectious bronchitis
  • Bacterial blood infections

Non-core shots are indicated by the activities you do and the places you go, for example time in woodsy or rural areas.

Cats

Core
  • Rabies
  • Feline panleukopenia (distemper)
  • Feline herpes virus and calicivirus (respiratory infections)
Non-core
  • Feline chlamydophila
  • Feline leukemia virus (FeLV)

For cats who spend a lot of time outdoors or around other cats, the clinic strongly urges the non-core vaccines.

Traveling with your pet

Health certificates, planned ahead

For international travel, most countries require a health certificate completed by a USDA-accredited veterinarian, and the clinic has one. You will also need proof of vaccination, and sometimes a microchip, blood tests or parasite treatment. Requirements differ by country, so contact the embassy or consulate first and call the clinic ahead of the visit.
