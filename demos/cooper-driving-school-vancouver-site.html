Pick the one that sounds like you. Most people start with a quiet first meeting rather than a lesson at their front door.

I get anxious driving

Fear and anxiety assessment. Tell your story, see the car, move slowly in a calm area. Some drivers never go faster than 10 km/h on day one.

I need to pass my test

Class 7 (first exam) and Class 5 (stricter second exam, with highway driving) test prep, plus road test day with a warm-up.

I am a new driver

New novice drivers are welcome. Build the base: car control, sign reading and the actual laws, at your pace.

I had a crash or close call

A 90-minute recovery session: rebuild the event, then drive the skills related to it and rebuild confidence.

ICBC road tests

Plan the whole test day

Two test levels, one calm routine.

Class 7 and 7L test prep

The first exam has many surprising requirements. Cooper suggests going through both ICBC books in detail and practising with an experienced local instructor.

Class 5, 5L and LDL test prep

A second-level test with stricter criteria: highway driving and conflict avoidance awareness. One drive gives you two lists: your habits that need changing, and the ICBC fault items to avoid.

Failed your Class 7?

Cooper has a guide on what to do next. Text your story and he will answer.

Road test day, hour by hour

  • Warm-upOne hour with Cooper before the test. ICBC time 3:00 means meeting at 2:00.
  • ArriveAt the test centre 15 minutes before your test time.
  • ExamUsually starts within 15 minutes of schedule and runs about 40 minutes (26 minutes driving).
  • PaperworkICBC paperwork afterwards takes about 15 minutes.
  • TotalExpect about 2.5 hours from start to finish.
Using Cooper's car for the test? You must take a 1.5-hour lesson at least a week before test day. It is Cooper's call whether you are safe to use the car. You can also use the same car you trained in.
About Cooper

An anxiety-aware driving instructor

Cooper worked with the Transport Canada Ergonomics Division on some of the earliest studies of cell phone use while driving, and brings a background in driver assessments and measurement tools to every lesson.

His teaching follows a four-level skills pyramid with a strong emphasis on the base: car control, 100% sign reading and the actual laws. The focus is real confidence, real competence and an honest look at the risks, especially for young drivers.

He has also done trauma training through the RSEA course conducted by Sue McNeil, and train-the-trainer work, and has worked with fleets, government, municipalities, skid school and new driver insurance programs.

Questions

Before you text

Why start at King Edward Station?

Most driving schools go to your home. Cooper prefers a super quiet area, which lowers strain and stress, and he recommends it for anyone learning to drive, especially someone getting back into driving.

How long is a session?

Sessions are 90 minutes. Packages on the pricing page include five or ten 90-minute drives.

I am very nervous. Do I have to drive?

No. Many people do not drive on the first visit, or drive only a little from the instructor seat. Cancelling or delaying is okay too.

Do you only teach tests?

No. Services include test prep for Class 5 and Class 7, young drivers, fear and anxiety assessment, post-crash recovery, regular driving lessons and new novice drivers.

Can I phone?

Cooper openly avoids phone calls. Text short or long questions on 236-998-4268, email, or WhatsApp.

What do I bring?

Your real licence or related ICBC documents, including a temporary licence or ICBC code number attached to an international licence if that applies.
