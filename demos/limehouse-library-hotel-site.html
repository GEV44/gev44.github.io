Eat & drink

From breakfast to a rooftop cocktail

Five places to sit down, all under one roof.

Restaurant

On the ground floor: continental and cooked breakfasts every morning, and international dishes for lunch and dinner from the chefs. The Page Restaurant offers a 3-course set menu, and there is an à la carte menu.

Afternoon tea

Finger sandwiches, homemade scones, cakes and pastries with tea and coffee, and a glass of champagne if you like. Menus for Kids (Fairy Tale), Vegan and Gluten-Free, and a Gentleman's afternoon tea.

Lobby bar

At the centre of the hotel, beside the library: morning coffee, afternoon tea or an evening cocktail, with paintings to look at. The menu runs from pink lemonade and themed cakes to chocolate scones and smoked salmon sandwiches.

Rooftop terrace bar

Signature cocktails, beer and wine, alcohol-free drinks and tasty snacks. A food menu, wine list and drink list are on the website.

Bakery

Old favourites rediscovered and something new to find, from the hotel's own bakery.

Wellness

Spa, Turkish bath, gym and sauna

Treatments take place in a relaxing space inspired by the Eastern breeze. Pre-booking is requested for spa packages, which are subject to availability. Couple massages are not available.

Massages

Swedish (30, 50 or 70 minutes), deep tissue (50 or 70), head massage and a 15-minute express massage on one area.

Turkish bath (hammam)

A 45-minute exfoliating scrub and foam massage on warm marble, or 2 hours of self-use with a waistcloth, towels, slippers, shampoo and shower gel. Bookable 10am to 7pm.

Limehouse Bliss pairs a 30-minute massage or hammam treatment with the sauna, gym, Turkish bath and afternoon tea. Limehouse Serenity pairs a massage or hammam treatment with the 3-course set menu.

Our story

A library, awakened

Limehouse Library has been awakened from an eighteen-year slumber to take an active role in London's cultural scene again. It now hosts a boutique hotel, a library, a venue for bespoke events and a school of the culinary arts.

A new library of masterworks by the writers, politicians, scientists and philosophers who inspired the rooms opened alongside the hotel, with shelves of books on gastronomy, history and music as well.

The hotel partners with Docklands Academy London on a Culinary Arts Programme, hosting students keen to learn the authentic tastes of Anatolia.

1881

Construction of the library begins.

1901

Limehouse Library opens, thanks to the journalist and philanthropist John Passmore Edwards.

2003

The library closes after more than a century of service.

2014

The Hazev Group obtains long-term tenure of the building.

2022

Restoration is completed and the Limehouse Library Hotel opens.
