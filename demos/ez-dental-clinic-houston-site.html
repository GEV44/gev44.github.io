Treatment guide

Plain-language summaries based on the clinic’s own service pages. Your dentist confirms what applies to you.

Dental implants — how they work

Implants are small titanium posts that act as artificial tooth roots. They are placed in the jawbone under local anesthesia, fuse with it over time, and a custom crown, bridge or denture is attached once healed. Unlike dentures they don’t slip or need adhesives, and because they integrate with the jawbone they help prevent bone loss. The process: consultation and planning (examination and 3D scans), implant placement, a healing period of a few months, then the final restoration.

All-on-X implants — a full arch on four to six implants

A minimally invasive technique that replaces a full arch of missing teeth: four to six implants are placed strategically to support a custom-made, fixed denture. It suits patients needing a full-arch replacement or those who wear removable dentures and want something more permanent. Benefits listed: natural-looking teeth, a fixed non-removable denture, better chewing, clear speech, and fewer implants than a conventional approach. Planning starts with X-rays and 3D scans.

Porcelain veneers — in two visits

Veneers are very thin, tooth-colored porcelain laminates bonded to the front of the teeth. They can address chipped, stained, gapped, worn, misshapen or slightly misaligned teeth. Only a thin layer of enamel is removed, shades are chosen to match, and a new smile can be completed in two dental visits.

Dental emergencies

Examples the clinic lists: knocked-out or dislodged tooth, cracked or fractured teeth, severe tooth pain, abscess, swollen or bleeding gums, oral trauma, and broken fillings or loose dentures. Call right away for a same-day visit; the team walks you through what to do before you arrive. For a knocked-out tooth their site suggests holding it by the crown only, rinsing gently, and bringing it in a container of warm milk.

Technology in the office

Intraoral scanners and 3D printing replace messy impressions with precise digital models, and an on-site dental lab keeps most of the process under one roof, so treatment moves faster. Dr. Rathi’s sister company, Get Implant®, also teaches dentists through live seminars and hands-on courses.

Quick answers

Do you take my insurance?

Most PPOs, adult Medicaid, Medicare and CHIP. Call the office to confirm your exact plan.

Can I get seen the same day?

Same-day appointments are offered for urgent cases.

What if I don’t have insurance?

CareCredit, Cherry, or an in-house payment plan arranged before treatment.

Which office should I choose?

Either one. The S Texas 6 office (77083) is open Mon–Fri with a shorter Wednesday and Saturday by appointment; the East Fwy office (77015) is open Mon–Fri 9am–5pm. Tell the assistant your preference and the team will confirm availability.

Do you only do implants?

No. Implants are Dr. Rathi’s focus, but the clinic also covers routine checkups, cleanings, fillings, crowns, bridges, dentures, whitening, veneers, extractions and emergencies.

What does the process look like for a smile makeover?

It starts with a conversation about your goals. Options include whitening, bonding, porcelain veneers and a virtual smile makeover so you can see a plan before treatment begins. The team explains every step as it happens.

Is there a way to see real results?

The clinic publishes a smile gallery of before-and-after photos and patient reaction videos on its website; individual results vary.

I’m a dentist — can I learn from Dr. Rathi?

Yes, through the sister company Get Implant®, which runs live seminars and hands-on implant courses. There is also a dentist referral form on the clinic’s website.

Do you work on weekends?

Saturday at the S Texas 6 office is by appointment; the East Fwy office is closed weekends. Both are closed Sunday.
