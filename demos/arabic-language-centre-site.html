How you get started

Four steps, none of them a commitment until you decide.

  1. Try a class. Join a free trial in a small group where speaking comes first. For children, the teachers use it to assess their level.
  2. Find your level. Arabic: a short interview with the head teacher. English: a free online placement test. Already read and write? You still start at Beginners I if you cannot yet speak.
  3. Choose a format. Group, semi-private or private; online or at the World Trade Centre; evening, daytime or intensive.
  4. Learn and collect certificates. Course materials are provided. Adults receive a certificate at the end of each level, children after three consecutive terms.

What sets the method apart

  • Proven for over 44 years. Tested and refined across four decades.
  • Levantine dialect focus. The most neutral and widely understood spoken Arabic.
  • Real-life communication. Task-based lessons built around real conversations.
  • Balanced skills. Listening, speaking, reading and writing together, with a strong focus on conversation.
  • Simplified and enjoyable. Logical, step-by-step, repetition with practical use.
  • Everyday vocabulary. Topics come from daily situations, so you can use them immediately.

Also on offer: Seasonal Arabic Courses for children and teens, Study Abroad in Dubai, English courses for adults and professionals, and Arabic for nurseries and schools.

A family legacy in Arabic education

As featured in Khaleej Times, ALC began in 1980 when Lebanese educator Maliha Wehbe opened the centre at the Dubai World Trade Centre and introduced her signature method: simplicity, cultural immersion and practical conversation.

Today her son Fadeel and grandson Georges keep refining the method. Proprietary materials such as the Business and Social Arabic series and the Arabic Alphabet Guide help students build confidence in real-world situations.

In 2025 ALC joined the International Association of Language Centres (IALC), the first member in Dubai, and added a new course, English for Business Professionals.

Before you ask

Which Arabic do you teach?

Spoken Levantine Arabic, widely understood across the Arab world. Modern Standard Arabic is mostly formal writing and news; many students add it later.

I can read and write but not speak. Which level?

Beginners I. The focus is speaking and conversation; the head teacher can run a short level interview.

Do I need the alphabet first?

No. You can start with spoken Arabic right away and add reading and writing as you go.

What if I miss a class?

Adults receive the class recording and notes, or can book a paid private make-up class. Children and teens get one fixed free make-up session per term.

Can we try before enrolling?

Yes, there is a free trial class, with no pressure and no commitment.

Are books included?

Course materials are provided by the centre.

How big are the classes?

Adult group classes typically have 6 to 10 students; children and teens 8 to 10. Small enough that everyone speaks every lesson.

Do you offer online classes?

Yes. Online and in-person options are both available, so you can pick the format that suits your schedule.

Is there a certificate?

Adults receive a certificate of achievement at the end of each level. Children and teens receive one after three consecutive terms.

Do you run classes in school holidays?

Yes, there are special intensive Seasonal Arabic Courses, and the centre also runs immersion programmes that mix language learning with cultural activities.

How old do children need to be?

From age 3 (Reception level) up to 16, through to GCSE and AS level, grouped by age and level.

What about the refund policy?

The centre has a strict refund and cancellation policy. Please read the terms or ask the office before enrolling.
